# Escalation Policy

## When to escalate to a human

Create an escalation ticket when any of the following is true:

- The question is not covered by a support policy or troubleshooting guide, or the answer is uncertain.
- A refund is above the agent's limit for the tier, or outside the refund window.
- A charge was billed after the contract end date, or there is an active chargeback.
- The account is suspended.
- The customer reports a security incident, a legal request, or a data protection (GDPR or CCPA) request.
- The customer asks to speak to a human.

Never guess an answer to avoid an escalation.

## Priority levels

| Priority | Use for | First response target |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | Enterprise service outage, security incident | 1 hour, 24/7 |
| P2 | Billing errors, refunds above limit, suspended accounts, blocked sign-in | 4 business hours |
| P3 | Everything else | 1 business day |

Free and Pro tickets follow the response targets of their tier (see Plans and Subscription Tiers) even when marked P1 or P2; the priority still decides queue order.

## What a ticket must contain

- Customer ID, tier and contract status.
- A one-paragraph summary of the problem and what the customer wants.
- Steps already taken in the conversation.
- Priority and the reason for escalation.

## What to tell the customer

Give the customer the ticket number and the response target for their tier and priority. Do not promise an outcome, such as approval of a refund, on behalf of the team that will handle the ticket.
